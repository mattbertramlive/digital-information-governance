AI Regulation Tracker

The AI Regulation Tracker is a dated status table for the rules that govern AI-influenced decisions and AI agents. Every row names its scope, its current status, and the date it was last reviewed against primary sources.

How this table works

Regulatory summaries rot quietly: a page written in spring states a deadline that a summer amendment moved. This tracker treats status as data. Each row is reviewed against the primary source and stamped with its review date, and rows are corrected the way a living standard is corrected: visibly, with the change noted in the site changelog. A row is only as current as its date says it is.

The deferral is not a reprieve. The Omnibus moved the EU AI Act's main high-risk deadlines to 2027 and 2028, but agents are being deployed now, and the logging and oversight the Act expects cannot be reconstructed later. The record either exists when the question comes, or it does not.

Watchlist

On the watchlist, pending verification against primary sources before they get a row: California frontier-model legislation, Brazil's Bill 2338, India's AI liability framework, and state bills in progress. A regime moves from the watchlist to the table only when its status can be stated from a primary source with a date.

Frequently asked questions

When do the EU AI Act high-risk obligations apply?

Under the 2026 Digital Omnibus, the main high-risk obligations were deferred to December 2, 2027 for stand-alone Annex III systems and August 2, 2028 for AI embedded in Annex I regulated products. Transparency duties applied in August 2026.

Which US AI laws are already in effect?

Texas's TRAIGA took effect January 1, 2026, and NYC Local Law 144 has required bias audits for automated employment decision tools since 2023. Colorado's SB 24-205 obligations take effect in 2026 with an amended date.

How often is this tracker updated?

Rows are reviewed monthly against primary sources, and each row carries the date it was last reviewed. Changes are noted in the site changelog.
